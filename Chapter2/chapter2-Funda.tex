\section[Trade-Based Money Laundering ]{\textbf{Trade-Based Money Laundering }}
%========================================================
Trade-Based Money Laundering (TBML) represents a complicated form of financial crime that entails moving and hiding illegally obtained funds through the use of legitimate trade activities across international borders. In contrast to traditional laundering techniques, which rely on transactions, TBML utilizes import and export systems, trade documents, shipping documents, and cross-border business operations to conceal the flow of illegally obtained money. With the growing use of AML and KYC laws, criminals have been increasingly adopting trade-based techniques due to their relative anonymity.
Complexity involved in global trade ecosystems along with involvement of many
financial bodies and transactional relationship between these bodies has made the
detection of Trade Based Money Laundering very hard for financial institutions.
Therefore, intelligent analytics that can detect hidden transactional relationships
and suspicious financial activity have become essential in Anti-Money Laundering

%========================================================
\subsection[Introduction to TBML]{\textbf{Introduction to TBML}}
%========================================================
Trade based money laundering typically refers to any act by which criminal
organizations manipulate international transactions of trade through their documentations, invoice values, shipping volumes, etc. so as to move illegal funds through
them but in such a way that looks legitimate in the eyes of global financial
systems. Such laundering being carried out as a part of regular importing/ exporting
operations makes the detection process very difficult.
In many TBML schemes, the collusion between the buyer and seller results in deliberate misrepresentation of the price of traded products to launder illegally acquired finances into the other country. The methods used by launderers include over invoicing, under invoicing, phony shipments, and double invoicing. Such laundering schemes leverage the complicated nature and huge volume of transactions involved in the international trading system, resulting in rule-based Anti-Money Laundering policies being ineffective in identifying any concealed financial relationships.

\subsection[Challenges in Detecting TBML]{\textbf{Challenges in Detecting TBML}}
%========================================================
TBML detection is very challenging due to the complexity and connectedness of current trade networks. TBML is different from ordinary financial fraud as the fraudulent actions are concealed within legitimate trade transactions making it difficult to differentiate the two. Organized criminal syndicates have learned to conceal their actions by using shell companies, intermediaries,
different countries, and multi-layered transaction structures which are characteristic of TBML. Traditional AML techniques depend solely on rule-based detection and single transaction analysis. The methods may be able to detect any suspicious pattern but they cannot uncover the relationship of entities participating in large scale trading networks.
Furthermore, an increased number of transactions combined with sophisticated laundering processes poses even more challenges in detecting TBML transactions. There are also legal limitations in terms of sharing sensitive financial information among organizations.

\subsection{Graph Attention Network v2 (GATv2)}

Graph Attention Network version 2 (GATv2) is adopted for capturing the spatial relations of connected roads via dynamic attention, while Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) is utilized to detect temporal changes and sequential patterns in traffic dynamics. Both methods enhance traffic predictions, analysis, and decision-making process.

\subsubsection{Introduction}

Graph Attention Network version 2 (GATv2) is an intelligent Graph Neural Network (GNN)
architecture that can learn on graphs through the use of dynamic attention mechanism.
This architecture has been developed to address the problem of static attention that was
prevalent in the earlier versions of GAT. Through its ability to dynamically identify the
importance of neighboring nodes when aggregating features, GATv2 is ideal for tasks
such as traffic prediction, recommendation system, social networks, fraud detection, and
navigation for autonomous vehicles. 

\subsubsection{Fundamental Concept of GATv2}

The fundamental concept behind GATv2 is the generation of node embeddings by
dynamically aggregating features from the neighboring nodes. In this process, the model
learns about which neighboring nodes are important and how best they should be
weighted to extract their features.

\subsubsection{Architecture and Working of GATv2}

The overall architecture and working process of GATv2 is illustrated in 
 Figure~\ref{fig:gatv2_architecture}.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{Figures/GATv2.png}
    \caption{GATv2 Architecture and Working}
    \label{fig:gatv2_architecture}
\end{figure}

GATv2 architecture involves graph inputs, feature transformation, attention mechanism, normalization,
neighbor aggregation, multi-head attention mechanism, and predictions generation.

\paragraph{Step 1: Input Graph:}

The process begins with a graph containing nodes, edges, and node feature vectors. Each node feature vector is represented as:

\[
h_i \in \mathbb{R}^{F}
\]

where \(h_i\) denotes the feature vector of node \(i\) and \(F\) represents the number of input features. In traffic prediction systems, nodes may represent road intersections while edges represent road connectivity.

\paragraph{Step 2: Linear Transformation:}

The input node features are transformed using a learnable weight matrix:

\begin{equation}
z_i = Wh_i
\end{equation}
where \(W\) is the learnable weight matrix and \(z_i\) is the transformed feature representation. This transformation improves feature learning before attention computation.

\paragraph{Step 3: Attention Score Computation:}

For every connected node pair \((i,j)\), the attention score is computed as:

\begin{equation}
e_{ij}=a^{T}\text{LeakyReLU}\left(W[h_i \Vert h_j]\right)
\end{equation}

where \(a\) is the learnable attention vector and \(e_{ij}\) represents the attention score between neighboring nodes.

\paragraph{Step 4: Softmax Normalization:}

The attention scores are normalized using the softmax function:

\begin{equation}
\alpha_{ij} = \frac{\exp(e_{ij})}{\sum_{k \in \mathcal{N}(i)} \exp(e_{ik})}
\end{equation}

where \(\alpha_{ij}\) denotes the normalized attention coefficient and \(\mathcal{N}(i)\) represents the neighboring nodes of node \(i\).

\paragraph{Step 5: Neighbor Aggregation:}

The neighboring node features are aggregated using weighted summation:

\begin{equation}
h_i^{\prime} = \sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}(i)} \alpha_{ij}Wh_j
\end{equation}

where \(h_i^{\prime}\) is the updated node representation.

\paragraph{Step 6: Multi-Head Attention:}

GATv2 employs multiple attention heads operating in parallel:

\begin{equation}
h_i^{\prime} =
\mathop{\Vert}_{k=1}^{K}
\sigma
\left(
\sum_{j \in \mathcal{N}(i)}
\alpha_{ij}^{(k)}W^{(k)}h_j
\right)
\end{equation}

where \(K\) represents the number of attention heads and \(\Vert\) denotes concatenation. Multi-head attention improves representation learning and robustness.

\paragraph{Step 7: Output Layer and Prediction:}

The final node embeddings are passed to the output layer for tasks such as node classification, graph classification, link prediction, and regression.

\subsubsection{Advantages of GATv2}

GATv2 introduces a dynamic attention mechanism that enhances feature
aggregation and representation learning as compared to the original GAT model. The
model automatically detects important neighboring nodes, works efficiently with irregular
graphs, and improves robustness through multi-head attention, making it appropriate for
traffic prediction and autonomous driving purposes.

\subsubsection{Limitations of GATv2}

Although the model offers numerous benefits, it uses significant computational
resources and memory due to attention mechanisms on big graphs. GATv2 models
require more time to train as compared to the smaller graph size and deep models of
GATv2 face challenges of over-smoothing and scaling issues with extremely large
databases.

\subsubsection{GATv2 vs GAT}

Graph Attention Network v2 (GATv2) is a more improved version of the
previous Graph Attention Network (GAT), addressing the challenge of static attention
mechanisms. Both architectures involve the usage of attention-based aggregations in the
representation learning of graphs

In the original GAT architecture, the attention mechanism is defined as:

\begin{equation}
e_{ij} = \text{LeakyReLU}\left(a^{T}[Wh_i \Vert Wh_j]\right)
\end{equation}

In GATv2, the order of operations is modified as:

\begin{equation}
e_{ij}=a^{T}\text{LeakyReLU}\left(W[h_i \Vert h_j]\right)
\end{equation}

\subsection{Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM)}

\subsubsection{Introduction to LSTM}

Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) represents a sophisticated design of Recurrent Neural Networks (RNN) that allows learning long-term dependencies within sequential data. This architecture was created specifically to deal with the issue of vanishing gradients experienced in conventional RNN architectures. LSTM has an ability to selectively remember, update, and forget information, which makes it suitable for traffic predictions, speech recognition, natural language processing, and other areas related to time series analysis.

\subsubsection{Fundamental Concept of LSTM}

Main goal of LSTM lies in storing the essential sequential data for long periods of
time via using of memory cells and gate mechanisms.

\subsubsection{Architecture and Working of LSTM}

The entire process of LSTM involves gate calculation, memory cell calculation, and hidden
state calculation

\subsubsection{Input Gate, Forget Gate, and Output Gate}
At each time step, the current input and previous hidden state are used to compute the input, forget, and output gates using sigmoid activation functions. These gates regulate information flow into and out of the memory cell.

The computation of the input gate, forget gate, and output gate within an LSTM cell is illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:lstm_gates}.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.65\textwidth]{Figures/LSTM_0}
    \caption{Computing the input gate, forget gate, and output gate in an LSTM model}
    \label{fig:lstm_gates}
\end{figure}




\vspace{-0.3cm}

The gates are mathematically represented as:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathbf{I}_t &= \sigma(\mathbf{X}_t \mathbf{W}_{xi} + \mathbf{H}_{t-1}\mathbf{W}_{hi} + \mathbf{b}_i)\\
\mathbf{F}_t &= \sigma(\mathbf{X}_t \mathbf{W}_{xf} + \mathbf{H}_{t-1}\mathbf{W}_{hf} + \mathbf{b}_f)\\
\mathbf{O}_t &= \sigma(\mathbf{X}_t \mathbf{W}_{xo} + \mathbf{H}_{t-1}\mathbf{W}_{ho} + \mathbf{b}_o)
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where \(\mathbf{I}_t\), \(\mathbf{F}_t\), and \(\mathbf{O}_t\) represent the input, forget, and output gates respectively, while \(\mathbf{X}_t\) and \(\mathbf{H}_{t-1}\) denote the current input and previous hidden state.

\subsubsection{Input Node}

The candidate memory information is computed using the \texttt{tanh} activation function as:

\begin{equation}
\tilde{\mathbf{C}}_t = \tanh(\mathbf{X}_t\mathbf{W}_{xc} + \mathbf{H}_{t-1}\mathbf{W}_{hc} + \mathbf{b}_c)
\end{equation}

where \(\tilde{\mathbf{C}}_t\) represents the candidate memory state.

The computation of the candidate memory state (input node) in an LSTM model is illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:lstm_input_node}.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.65\textwidth]{Figures/LSTM_1}
    \caption{Computing the input node in an LSTM model}
    \label{fig:lstm_input_node}
\end{figure}

\subsubsection{Memory Cell Internal State}

The memory cell state is updated using the forget gate and input gate as:

\begin{equation}
\mathbf{C}_t = \mathbf{F}_t \odot \mathbf{C}_{t-1} + \mathbf{I}_t \odot \tilde{\mathbf{C}}_t
\end{equation}

where \(\mathbf{C}_t\) represents the updated memory cell state and \(\odot\) denotes element-wise multiplication. This mechanism helps preserve important long-term information while discarding irrelevant data.

The computation of the memory cell internal state within an LSTM cell is shown in Figure~\ref{fig:lstm_memory_cell}.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.65\textwidth]{Figures/LSTM_2}
    \caption{Computing the memory cell internal state in an LSTM model}
    \label{fig:lstm_memory_cell}
\end{figure}


\subsubsection{Hidden State}

The hidden state output is computed as:

\begin{equation}
\mathbf{H}_t = \mathbf{O}_t \odot \tanh(\mathbf{C}_t)
\end{equation}
where \(\mathbf{H}_t\) represents the hidden state at time step \(t\). The output gate controls how much information from the memory cell is passed to the next layer.

The computation of the hidden state in an LSTM cell is shown in Figure~\ref{fig:lstm_hidden_state}.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.65\textwidth]{Figures/LSTM_3}
    \caption{Computing the hidden state in an LSTM model}
    \label{fig:lstm_hidden_state}
\end{figure}

\subsubsection{Advantages of LSTM}

LSTM is able to effectively tackle the vanishing gradient problem and to capture long-term sequential dependencies through memory cells and gating mechanisms. It works well on sequential and time-series data and is employed in such fields as traffic prediction, speech recognition, machine translation and forecasting.
\subsubsection{Limitations of LSTM}

Although beneficial, LSTM is a costly algorithm for computations involving more than one gate and memory operation. It is more expensive to train and takes longer to run, and it may be less accurate when the number of frames is very large or is too small to train.

\section{Federated Learning}

Federated Learning (FL) is a decentralized machine learning technique that involves multiple devices or organizations training a shared model without sharing raw data. Local training is done on each client's data, and only model parameters are shared with a central server to provide privacy-preserving collaborative learning in distributed environments.

\subsection{Introduction}
raditional machine learning systems rely on centralized training, where data from multiple users or organizations is collected and stored on a central server for model development. Although effective, this approach raises significant concerns related to data privacy, security, and regulatory compliance. Federated Learning addresses these challenges by enabling collaborative model training without sharing confidential or sensitive data. In Trade-Based Money Laundering (TBML) detection systems, organizations such as banks and financial institutions can locally train models using transaction records and suspicious trade patterns while sharing only model parameters with a central server. Introduced by Google in 2016 for Gboard applications, Federated Learning has emerged as an important privacy-preserving approach for distributed machine learning systems.

The overall architecture and workflow of Federated Learning are illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:fl_overview}.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.9\linewidth]{Figures/FL_0.png}
    \caption{General Outlook of Federated Learning}
    \label{fig:fl_overview}
\end{figure}

% \textit{Source: Adapted from ``Principles and Components of Federated Learning Architectures" 
% \href{https://arxiv.org/html/2502.05273v1}{Arxiv}.}

 Federated Learning decreases communication overhead since
communication happens through model updates rather than full datasets. Thus, federated learning is ideal for large-scale distributed settings such as mobile
devices, Internet of Things systems, edge devices, and financial institution\cite{federated-arxiv}.

\subsection{Working of Federated Learning}

Federated Learning uses an iterative client-server protocol where both client and
server perform rounds of interaction, commonly referred to as federated rounds. In each round, the central server sends the existing global model to chosen
clients, who train independently on their local data. Then, model parameters are aggregated to produce a new improved global model

Figure~\ref{fig:fl_workflow} illustrates the federated
learning working procedure, including global model distribution, independent local training, model parameters aggregation, and generation of global model improvements.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{Figures/FL_1.png}
    \caption{Federated Learning Workflows}
    \label{fig:fl_workflow}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Step 1: Initialization}

A preliminary machine learning model is chosen and trained at the central server, while other client machines or institutional servers
are ready for local training.

\paragraph{Step 2: Client Selection}

Client Selection Only a subset of clients is selected during each federated
round and provided with updated global model parameters sent by the central server.

\paragraph{Step 3: Local Training}

Each selected client trains the received model locally using its private dataset without sharing raw transactional or customer information.

\paragraph{Step 4: Reporting and Aggregation}

After local training, clients send only model parameters or gradients to the aggregation server. The server combines these updates using aggregation algorithms such as Federated Averaging (FedAvg).

The Federated Averaging process is represented as:

\begin{equation}
w_{t+1} = \sum_{k=1}^{K} \frac{n_k}{n} w_k^{(t+1)}
\end{equation}

where \(w_{t+1}\) represents the updated global model, \(w_k^{(t+1)}\) denotes the local model of client \(k\), \(n_k\) represents the number of training samples at client \(k\), and \(n\) denotes the total number of training samples across all clients.

\paragraph{Step 5: Termination}

The federated training process continues iteratively until the desired accuracy, convergence criteria, or maximum communication rounds are achieved

\subsection{Advantages of Federated Learning}

Federated Learning improves data privacy and security by ensuring that raw data remains within local devices or organizations. It supports regulatory compliance, enables collaborative intelligence without direct data sharing, reduces communication overhead, and improves model generalization using diverse distributed datasets. FL is also scalable and supports real-time learning on edge devices and organizational servers. 

\subsection{Limitations of Federated Learning}

Despite its advantages, Federated Learning introduces challenges such as communication delays, high computational requirements, non-i.i.d. data distributions, client failures, synchronization issues, and security threats such as model poisoning and backdoor attacks. Variations in client hardware capabilities and unstable network connections can also affect training efficiency and global model convergence.

\section{User Interface}
%========================================================

Contemporary financial monitoring solutions use the rapidly evolving web application technologies as their basis. Increasing demand for responsive, scalable and data-driven monitoring solutions in the face of growing popularity of digital banking and financial services led to creation of scalable web application technologies designed to provide an efficient and fast transaction analysis, fraud monitoring and visual presentation of financial activities. As such, contemporary anti-money laundering solutions leverage component-based web architecture and client-server communication technology as a basis for providing efficient and scalable interaction and analytics. Frontend frameworks and backend service architectures enable the development of modular software solutions capable of supporting dynamic rendering, authentication and integration with the underlying fraud detection engine. This approach allows to visualize suspicious activities, generate alerts, provide access control based on user roles as well as automate compliance tasks.

%========================================================
\subsection[Modern Web Application Frameworks]{\textbf{Modern Web Application Frameworks}}
%========================================================

Modern web application frameworks represent architectural foundations for developing responsive, scalable and interactive applications. Contrary to traditional web applications which were predominantly built using static pages and tightly-coupled server-side architecture, modern web application frameworks utilize component-based architecture and asynchronous rendering approaches in order to increase the scalability, maintainability and efficiency of user interface in distributed environment. 

The use of frontend frameworks like React.js allows creating reusable and modular user
interface elements that can change their state without reloading the entire web page. The
virtual DOM is the key principle behind these frameworks, allowing for fast and smooth user
interaction and visualization of real-time data. This approach ensures that applications
respond quickly to any changes and improves their performance. Thus, such frameworks
allow scalable dashboard implementation for data heavy apps like financial monitoring.

The combination of client-side interaction and server-side content generation in
hybrid web frameworks such as Next.js helps to make web applications highly performant.
These frameworks ensure optimal routing and data fetching and allow improving
application scalability. Therefore, modern frameworks became a basis for creating secure,
scalable, and real-time financial monitoring platforms


%========================================================
\subsection[Component Lifecycle and Reactivity]{\textbf{Component Lifecycle and Reactivity}}
%========================================================
Reactive rendering techniques are employed by contemporary frontend frameworks like React and Next.js in order to keep user interfaces synchronized with the constantly changing state and backend response of applications. In the case of the proposed TBML detection framework, it allows for effective visualization of transaction analysis data, fraud alerts, compliance operations, and real-time monitoring data without the need to reload the entire interface. Component-based architecture also provides enhanced scalability, maintainability, and responsiveness when dealing with large-scale financial monitoring systems.
Component lifecycle typically includes rendering, initializing state, reactivity, synchronization, and cleanup procedures while performing. Major stages in the lifecycle related to the proposed dashboard architecture include the following:

\vspace{0.3cm}

\noindent\textbf{Server-Side Rendering:}  
First, the rendering of dashboard components is performed at the server side, which processes transactions summaries, analysis data, and monitoring information for further transmission to the client's web browser. This approach allows for faster initial load times and improved search engine optimization (SEO) for the financial monitoring application.
\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent\textbf{Hydration and Client Initialization:}  
Once the interface is rendered in the browser, React's hydration mechanism allows the creation of responsive client-side functionality such as events handling, asynchronous API communication, initial state setup, and real-time dashboard services.

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent\textbf{Reactive State Updates:}  
In accordance with changes that take place in transaction states, fraud alerts, and analytics, React updates only those parts of the interface that have been altered by employing the Virtual DOM reconciliation approach, as opposed to redrawing the whole dashboard.

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent\textbf{Synchronizing Dynamically:}
 React continually synchronizes changes to transactions, compliance alerts, and monitoring analytics for use in fraud visualizations and consistent analytics on the dashboard.

\vspace{0.2cm}

\noindent\textbf{ Cleanup and Unmounting:}  
As components are unmounted from the interface, temporary states, subscriptions, and allocated resources will be freed up automatically.
This is critical in a world of constantly changing transactional data, fraud alerts, and analysis visualizations.

\vspace{0.2cm}

Such lifecycle management principles are important within modern financial monitoring systems where continuous transactional updates, fraud notifications, and analytical visualizations must be processed efficiently while maintaining scalable and responsive user interaction.
